Grading Engine

How every site gets its score: what data feeds in, how it's weighted, and why the benchmarks are set where they are.

OverviewThe two-part score

Every site carries two independent scores that combine into one number:

Macro · 60% weight

Is the neighbourhood right? Pulled from ONS Census 2021 data via the Homedata API — no AI involved, pure statistics.

Micro · 40% weight

Is the street right? Claude reads the location's context and rates footfall, visibility and findability.

Combined score = Macro × 0.6 + Micro × 0.4

Both scores run 1–5. A site never needs both to show a number — the combined score falls back to whichever one has run — but the full pipeline score always uses both.

Macro scoreDemographic catchment

The macro score asks one question: does this postcode's MSOA (a Census geography of ~7,000–8,000 people) look like the neighbourhoods our members already live in? Seven signals are pulled for the postcode, each compared against a benchmark, then combined into a weighted score.

SignalWeightBenchmarkDirection
Professionals (managerial/professional occupation, %)30%66%Higher is better
Deprivation / affluence score22%4.0Higher is better
Absolute professional headcount in MSOA15%7,100Higher is better
Age 30–44 (%)12%40%Higher is better
Age 16–29 (%)8%26%Higher is better

How the maths works

1
Ratio against benchmark. Each signal is divided by its benchmark (for social rent, this is flipped — benchmark ÷ actual — since less social renting scores higher).
2
Ratio converted to a step score. Ratios don't scale linearly — a site has to be reasonably close to the benchmark to score well, then it plateaus:
Ratio vs. benchmarkStep score
≥ 1.10 (10%+ above benchmark)1.00
≥ 0.900.80
≥ 0.750.55
≥ 0.550.30
below 0.550.05
3
Weighted average. Each step score is multiplied by its signal's weight and summed. If a signal is missing for a postcode (rare, but happens on data gaps), it's dropped and the remaining weights re-normalise, so a missing signal never artificially caps the score.
4
Mapped to 1–5. The weighted average (0–1) is stretched onto the 1–5 scale and rounded to one decimal place.
Macro score = clamp(1, 5, round( (1 + W × 4) × 10 ) / 10)

Micro scoreStreet quality and footfall

The macro score can't tell a bustling high street from a quiet side road one postcode away — that's what the micro score is for. It's a blend of three components, each 1–5:

ComponentWeightWhat it measures
Volume30%Total review volume of nearby venues — a proxy for how much foot traffic the area generally sees.
Intent35%Count of nearby venues whose customers overlap with ours (gyms, bars, coworking, etc. within 400m).
Character35%Claude's read of the street itself: is it a through-route with passing trade, or a quiet side street near a busier area?

For the Character component, Claude is given the actual businesses found on that specific street plus the positive/negative venues within 400m, and rates it against this rubric:

1Hidden — residential or industrial, no passing trade
2Quiet side street, low visibility
3Moderate passing trade
4Busy through-route, active in the evenings
5Exceptional footfall on the street itself
The Character prompt is explicitly anchored on our own member research: 41% of current members say they discovered their site by walking past it. That's the reason street-level visibility carries real weight here rather than being a minor tiebreaker — for this business, the street is a genuine acquisition channel, not just ambience.

The benchmarksWhy Balham & Wandsworth set the bar

Every macro benchmark is the blended average of live Homedata pulls for both sites' postcodes — Balham (SW12 9EL) and Wandsworth (SW18 1UP). A benchmark ratio of 1.0 doesn't mean "good," it means "matches what we already know works." Pulled fresh from the same Homedata endpoints the engine itself calls:

Six of the seven benchmarks line up almost exactly with a live blended pull — reassuring confirmation the code constants were genuinely built from these two sites' real data, not guessed. The one exception is deprivation: the current code benchmark of 4 sits well below today's live blended figure of 7. In practice this means the deprivation signal is currently maxed out (step score 1.00) for almost any comparably affluent London site, since even a moderately-deprived-relative postcode clears a benchmark of 4 easily. Worth a deliberate decision — either the benchmark was set against an earlier Homedata scale that's since changed, or it was intentionally set as a low floor rather than a like-for-like average. Either way it's not currently doing much discriminating work at 22% weight.

How this ties back to the onboarding form

The benchmark table above answers "does this postcode look statistically like Balham or Wandsworth?" — but the onboarding form analysis (53 member responses) is what tells us whether that's actually the right question, and where the formula's current assumptions and real behaviour diverge:

Onboarding findingWhat it validates or challenges
Occupations cluster in Finance, Consulting, Sales, Software EngineeringValidates the 30% weight on Professionals — the single largest signal in the formula matches the single clearest pattern in the actual member base.
Age sweet spot is 28–40, tighter than the original 25–40 design assumptionValidates weighting Age 30–44 (12%) above Age 16–29 (8%) — most members sit inside the older band, not spread evenly across both.
Real catchment radius is ~1km, not the "10–15 min walk" (~1.2–1.3km) the abs. professionals signal was scoped againstChallenges the neighbourhood-professionals signal slightly — it may be counting a marginally wider ring than members actually travel from. A candidate for tightening once there's time to rebuild that MSOA-catchment query.
Balham draws almost entirely from SW12; Wandsworth draws from SW18, SW11 and SW4Challenges the formula more fundamentally — it scores a postcode's own demographics, not how far people are willing to travel to reach it. A site that demographically resembles Wandsworth may support a wider, thinner catchment than one resembling Balham, and nothing in the macro or micro score currently captures that difference.

In short: the benchmark numbers are correctly calibrated against the two live sites' areas. The onboarding form is calibrated against the two live sites' members — and it's the second dataset that will matter most once we're evaluating Clapham/Battersea candidates that don't cleanly resemble either existing site.

Micro: same site

Volume 3/5 (moderate review density nearby) · Intent 4/5 (several compatible venues within 400m) · Character 3/5 (Claude reads it as moderate passing trade, not a dead street but not a high street either)

Micro score = round(3 × 0.30 + 4 × 0.35 + 3 × 0.35) = round(3.35) = 3 / 5

Combined

Combined = 3.2 × 0.6 + 3 × 0.4 = 1.92 + 1.2 = 3.1 / 5

A 3.1 sits below both benchmark sites on demographics, but not badly — it's the kind of score worth a human look rather than an automatic reject, particularly if the micro breakdown shows genuine passing trade rather than a fluke of nearby review counts.
